For most of you, it first gurgled out around the time you were just one year old. Though you'll try and fail to recall the miraculous moment, there's a small chance its inception remains memorialized on some grainy videotape that's been relegated to the back of a vinyl-lined drawer. Ever since, it has only stayed loyal as a vocal friend, even if you've conveniently misplaced the relative importance and conscious awareness of its existence. A most amazing possession, I speak, of course, of your voice.

Words well-spoken command immense influence. They have the ability to precipitate the [successful siege of Orléans and victory for France in the Hundred Years' War][joan], or even [solidify the movement for South African democracy][mandela]. Precise phrases adeptly weather the listener's consciousness and unconsciousness, and the pithiest demonstrate *remarkable* persistence:

> "Give me liberty, or give me death!" - Patrick Henry, 1775

> ![Ode to Joy spectrogram](/assets/images/speech/ode_to_joy.jpg)
> 
> \- Spectrogram of Ode to Joy, Ludwig van Beethoven, 1785

The claims above hold true for a live audience listening to analog sounds, yet curiously, when a voice becomes embodied, so too can its power. In the same manner that a great speaker is in the business of ideas, a great author is involved in the trade of thoughts. Consider an [archaic treatise on war that's simultaneously a timeless treatise on life][sunzi], demonstrating through its duality and superior coverage how indeed,

> "The pen is mightier than the sword." - Edward Bulwer-Lytton, 1839

We conclude that a powerful payload of insight and emotion can be surgically inserted through either the eyes or the ears. There are brief moments when each of us is afforded the opportunity to carefully arrange the correct phrases into the correct ideas to be transmitted to a receptive audience. As someone with ideas, especially important ones that you'd like to propagate, you should not neglect exercising, improving, and refining your voice. **In a world full of noise, eloquence and cogency are necessary for garnering support for your initiatives, and in doing so, increase your likelihood of success.**

## How to sing (on paper)

### 1. Be novel

Humans are known to direct attention to novel stimuli {% cite Berlyne1950Sep Bradley2009Jan Itti2009Jun %}. In contrast, the mental processing resources allocated to repetitive stimuli are mitigated. When your purpose is to convey an idea or apply rhetoric, ask yourself first,

> "Does what I'm about to write merit thoughtful attention?"

Keep in mind that not all attention is useful in establishing your intellectual voice. There are already many voices out there. Nearly everyone can make a Twitter account and say *something*. Instead, expend the necessary effort to repeatedly produce valuable insights in order to increase your signal to noise ratio.

In 2017, I found myself sipping coffee with Ken Liu, author of *The Paper Menagerie* and English translator for Liu Cixin's renowned *The Three Body Problem*. Having no connection with him before, he graciously accepted an offer to meet after work in response to a casual email I sent a month previously (I still can't believe it actually happened). During that meeting, he produced an excellent phrase:

> "Find your own flavor or taste." - Ken Liu, 2017

Write about whatever excites you. This is how you produce good work. Ken, a multiple Hugo and Nebula award winner himself, emphasized how literary prizes and awards are random. He instead encourages new writers to evaluate success by correlating it with their own sense of continued interest in their pursuits. An author is inevitably expressed in their work, and through feedback and practice, you too will accumulate the skills and reputation required for commanding even greater audiences in the future.

### 2. Be accurate

Writing without anticipating your readers' questions and responses leaves large rhetorical holes that make it all too easy to dismiss your ideas out of hand. Fortunately, none of us lives in a vacuum, and there already exists an immense body of scientific work to draw upon in support of your claims. According to Garfield, citations may be used to substantiate claims, provide background reading, and criticize previous work, along with 12 other reasons {% cite Garfield64cancitation %}. A more modern study by Bornmann and Hans-Dieter extends the analysis to consider sociological influences that explain citation behavior {% cite Bornmann2008Jan %}.  Personally, I like to think of citations as scientific duct tape. Properly applied, citations can offer incredible utility by adding dimensionality to your work even outside of academic writing. 

Again, Ken had some wisdom to share from his own experience with research and citations. Perhaps unsurprisingly, as a science fiction author, he dedicates a sizeable amount of time poring over actual science. Regarding the hours and hours digging into the various topics required to support a coherent story, he simply stated that

> "Sometimes, it can consume you." - Ken Liu, 2017

Reflecting on his own writing, Ken mentioned that fiction itself is not a good way to convince others; it serves instead as a house for readers to explore at their leisure. With similar thoughtfulness, he also expressed doubt that science journalism effectively conveys the actual science to the masses. The careful reader will notice that Ken provides a works cited page at the end of certain short stories for his audience to come to their own conclusions. Let the players play, so to speak.

Hopefully you're excited about ~~duct tape~~ citations now, but before throwing a bunch of links behind your major points with the expectation that some poor reader will be convinced through sheer volume, understand that scientific discernment is a skill. This means that you will suck at it without practice, and even with practice, you will make mistakes every so often. 

> "Not all scientific studies are created equal." - David H. Schwartz, 2014

However, it is a skill you *must* practice. To be independent, to guide others and aim higher, begin by securing your own analytical ability. Engage with acknowledged thinkers and derive from those interactions key lessons whenever you are humbled. Share your own critical thoughts and be ready to reevaluate your footing whenever there's pushback. Through practice, you will increase both the actions available to you and your ability to decide between them all.

## Your greatest tool

Without waxing poetic, I'll end with this: after you've determined the ideas you find valuable, or even if you need help assessing their worth... put them out there! Go ahead, take the time to drape them with intrigue and fortify them with citations, but after everything is prepared, you must do. You must choose to take action as only you can in an arena of your choosing. [Start a blog][blog]! [Lead a reading group][wu]! [Write to your legislators][aclu]!

With the proper presentation, you will find support. There is always a leap at the end, and sometimes the platform on the other side isn't so clear. Agency is only deciding to jump and land *somewhere*.
